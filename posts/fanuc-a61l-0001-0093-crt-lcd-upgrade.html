Do You Need a FANUC A61L-0001-0093 LCD Replacement? (Diagnose First)

Before you spend a dollar on a FANUC A61L-0001-0093 LCD replacement, confirm the screen is the fault. Many "dead display" calls turn out to be a loose cable, a blown fuse, or a mainboard issue — none fixed by a new CRT or LCD. Every fanuc 0 crt display repair and fanuc 0 crt no display troubleshoot job comes down to one rule: isolate the display before you condemn it.

The tell-tale sign of an aged tube is gradual dimming. FANUC 9-inch CRTs lose roughly 40% of their brightness over their service life. The aging curve is brutal: 0–5 years you retain 100–85% brightness; 5–8 years drops to 85–60%; 8–12 years to 60–35%; beyond 12 years you're below 35%, and that's when the flashlight trick stops working.

A quick CRT-fault vs CNC/mainboard-fault decision tree

Work top-down before you condemn the display:

  1. Does the control power on? If not, check the AC 220V supply, the fuse, and the PSU — a dead PSU means no clean 24V reaches the display.
  2. Is the display's indicator light on? If the panel LED is dark, reseat the HONDA 20-pin and DC 24V leads; a half-seated connector mimics a dead tube.
  3. Is brightness present? Confirm the 6.3 V heater and 5 V logic rails. Good rails but a dim/dark screen means the tube is the fault — adjust or replace.
  4. Is the image normal? Brightness but a garbled or missing picture points to CNC/software, not the display.
  5. Is sync stable? Vertical/horizontal roll means HSYNC/VSYNC trouble; if cables check out and sync won't lock, replace the display.

Only after steps 1–5 point at the tube should you commit to a display replacement.

When (temporary) repair beats replacement

Not every fault warrants a swap. If the tube is young (under ~5 years, still bright) and you're seeing sync-roll or flicker from a tired flyback, a component-level CRT repair can buy years. But repeat repairs run $995–$1,990 each — a recurring cost on a 15,000-hour part. Once a tube is burned-in or past 8–12 years, replacement (or the LCD upgrade) is the cheaper, permanent answer.

⚠️ Safety First — High-Voltage Hazards

This is the section most guides skip and the one that matters most. A CRT is not a harmless panel — it's a small television tube with a capacitor on the anode that retains 15–28 kV after you've switched the machine off. That charge can kill. Treat every CRT as live until proven discharged. (Our lockout procedure follows the OSHA Control of Hazardous Energy standard, 29 CFR 1910.147.)

The 15–28 kV anode capacitor & why LOTO is mandatory

The anode cap — the suction-cup connector on the tube's side — sits at up to 28 kV and holds a dangerous residual charge long after power-off. LOTO isn't paperwork here: it's the only thing between you and a lethal shock with your hands inside the bezel. Isolate the AC at the source, tag it, and keep it isolated until the swap is done.

Safe discharge & lockout procedure before touching the tube

Safely discharging the CRT anode takes six steps. Follow this order, every time:

  1. Power down the CNC and disconnect the AC supply at the breaker or isolation switch.
  2. Lock out and tag out the disconnect. One person, one lock, one key.
  3. Wait at least 5–10 minutes for the residual charge to bleed (longer is safer).
  4. Discharge the anode with a proper insulated high-voltage probe/resistor lead: clip the grounded lead to the CRT chassis ground, then carefully slide the probe under the anode cap to short it to ground. You should hear/see a small discharge. Repeat to confirm.
  5. Verify with a multimeter that the anode and any exposed HV points read ~0 V before you touch anything.
  6. Only then remove the 4 mounting screws and disconnect the HONDA 20-pin and DC 24V leads.

If you are not trained in HV discharge, stop and bring in a qualified technician. The ~$200 LCD upgrade removes this hazard entirely — the KONGTO unit runs on DC 12–24V from the same 24V rail, with no high-voltage section at all.

FANUC A61L-0001-0093 LCD Replacement: Step-by-Step Teardown

The FANUC A61L-0001-0093 LCD replacement takes five steps and about 15 minutes. This is the core teardown, ordered step by step. Hands-on time is about 15–20 minutes once the tube is safe to handle (up to 30 with discharge and verification). Difficulty: intermediate — hand tools and LOTO, no electronics background needed.

Step 1 — Power down & lockout the CNC

Switch off the controller, pull the AC disconnect, and apply your LOTO lock and tag. Wait 5–10 minutes for the HV capacitor to bleed, then discharge the anode per the safety procedure and verify ~0 V with your multimeter. Never skip this step.

Step 2 — Remove the old CRT

With the tube confirmed safe, unscrew the four mounting screws holding the CRT bezel. The tube weighs ~2–3 kg — support it with two hands and a helper as you ease it forward. Disconnect the HONDA 20-pin and the DC 24V lead, then set the old tube aside; CRT glass is fragile.

Signal & Frequency Primer — Why It's Plug-and-Play

Engineers reasonably worry that an LCD won't "speak" the old CRT's video language. With this unit, it does — here's the technical reason.

The HONDA 20-pin interface

The HONDA 20-pin connector (HRS blue shell) carries everything the display needs: GND, R, G, B, INTENSITY, HSYNC, VSYNC, and DC 24V. There's no separate video board, no external scaler, no LVDS bridge to wire. The KONGTO LCD accepts that exact pinout on the same connector.

TTL/RGB (CGA) monochrome video, 640×480, ~15.7 kHz

FANUC 9-inch CRTs in the A61L-0001-009X family use TTL/RGB monochrome signaling at a line frequency of ~15.7 kHz (TTL spans 15.7–24 kHz, RGB 15.7–31.5 kHz) and a frame rate of 50–60 Hz. The native CRT resolution is 640×400, accepting up to 640×480 over RGB. The KONGTO panel internally converts that old signal path (TTL/RGB → VGA → TFT-LCD) and upscales to 800×600 on a modern TFT. The control side sees no difference.

(For reference, 14-inch FANUC variants use CGA/EGA rather than this TTL/RGB scheme — a different signal family and not compatible with this 9-inch swap.)

Why no adapter or CNC parameter change is needed

Because the conversion happens entirely inside the KONGTO unit, the CNC mainboard still outputs the same HONDA 20-pin TTL/RGB it always did — no parameter to edit, no video mode to select, no "new monitor" handshake. Your machine parameters, offsets, and programs stay exactly as they are. That's the whole point of a drop-in LCD.
