Pros and Cons of Each Converter Type

GBS-8219 —CGA/EGA/MDA to VGA Multi-Format Converter

The GBS-8219 is the most versatile converter for legacy CNC systems that use pre-VGA video standards. It accepts TTL-level CGA, EGA, MDA, and even IBM 5150 signals and outputs standard analog VGA (RGBHV).

  • Pros:
    • Supports the widest range of legacy video formats —CGA (15.75kHz), EGA (21.8kHz), MDA (18.4kHz), and custom multi-sync signals up to 64kHz
    • Auto-detects input scan rate and adjusts output timing automatically —no manual dip-switch configuration needed for most signals
    • Wide operating temperature range (-20C to +60C), suitable for unventilated machine-tool enclosures
    • Compact metal housing provides EMI shielding in electrically noisy factory environments
    • Powers via USB or 5V DC adapter —flexible installation options
  • Cons:
    • Output resolution limited to 1280x1024 maximum —cannot drive 1080p or higher resolution displays natively
    • Requires knowledge of your CNC's exact video output standard —selecting the wrong input mode can produce no image or a scrambled display
    • More expensive ($60-$120) than simple HDMI-to-VGA adapters due to the multi-format decoding chipset
    • Not needed if your CNC already outputs standard VGA —would be overkill
    • No audio extraction or embedding —signal conversion only

KT-HDMI-VGA —HDMI to VGA + Audio Converter

This converter is designed for the reverse signal path: taking a modern HDMI source and driving a VGA-only display. It is essential when a PC-based HMI, modern laptop, or digital media source must connect to an industrial display that only has a VGA port.

  • Pros:
    • Preserves full 1920x1080 HD resolution —text, axis readouts, and DRO displays remain crisp and readable
    • Built-in audio extraction via 3.5mm jack —useful when driving displays without speakers while still needing sound for alarms or operator prompts
    • EDID passthrough ensures the source device correctly identifies the display's capabilities, preventing blank screens or wrong resolution
    • Plug-and-play —no configuration required. HDMI source auto-detects the converter as a display
    • Ultra-compact form factor (60x45x15mm) fits inside control panels with limited space
  • Cons:
    • Cannot convert CGA/EGA or any sub-VGA signal —digital HDMI input only
    • Limited operating temperature range (-10C to +50C) —not suitable for unventilated enclosures near hot machinery without additional cooling
    • Some inexpensive HDMI-to-VGA cables on the market skip the HDCP handshake —the KT-HDMI-VGA includes proper HDCP support but compatibility varies with some source devices
    • Output is analog VGA —long cable runs (over 15m / 50ft) will show signal degradation; use a VGA booster or active extension for longer distances

KT-VGA-HDMI —VGA to HDMI Upscaler

This converter solves the opposite problem: taking an analog VGA signal from legacy equipment and driving a modern HDMI display. It includes an upscaling engine to match the source resolution to the display's native panel resolution.

  • Pros:
    • Upscales VGA input from as low as 640x480 up to 1920x1080 HDMI output —useful when connecting older CNC controllers to modern 1080p or 4K monitors
    • Audio embedding —can inject audio into the HDMI stream for displays with built-in speakers
    • Preserves 1:1 pixel mapping option —if you prefer to see the original VGA resolution centered on the HDMI display without scaling artifacts
    • Compatible with standard 31.5kHz VGA timing used by most post-2000 CNC controllers from FANUC, Siemens, Okuma, and Haas
    • Sturdy aluminum housing with mounting tabs for panel installation
  • Cons:
    • Upscaling can introduce 1-2 frames of latency (typically ~16-33ms) —generally imperceptible for CNC display use but noticeable in real-time video or gaming applications
    • Does not accept CGA/EGA/MDA signals —only standard VGA (RGBHV, 31.5kHz-60kHz)
    • Slightly more expensive than passive VGA cables —the ADC and scaler chipset adds $15-$25 to the cost
    • Some very old VGA sources (pre-1995) output non-standard sync timing that may not lock correctly; check compatibility with your specific controller model

Selection Criteria by Application

For FANUC CNC Systems

Choose GBS-8219 for converting CGA/EGA signals from older FANUC systems to modern VGA displays. Supports 9" CRT replacement displays with native resolution. For FANUC Series 16 and newer that output standard VGA, a direct VGA cable or KT-VGA-HDMI (if using an HDMI display) is the appropriate choice.

For Siemens/Mitsubishi Systems

Choose KT-VGA-HDMI when legacy VGA output needs to connect to modern HDMI displays or monitors. For Siemens Sinumerik 810/840D, verify the sync polarity —these systems use separate H and V sync (RGBHV), which the KT-VGA-HDMI handles correctly.

For HMI Integration

Choose KT-HDMI-VGA when modern HDMI-enabled HMI panels (e.g. Siemens Comfort Panels, Weintek cMT, or PC-based HMI software) need to connect to VGA input industrial displays. The built-in audio extraction is particularly useful when the display lacks speakers but audio alerts are required.

For Pre-1995 CNC Systems (Any Brand)

If your machine was manufactured before 1995, there is a high probability it uses CGA, EGA, or a proprietary multi-sync video standard rather than VGA. The GBS-8219 is the only converter in this guide that can handle these legacy signals. Always verify the original monitor's pinout and sync type before purchasing.
