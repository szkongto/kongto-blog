Before You Replace: Diagnose the Fault

Before spending on a replacement, confirm the CRT itself is the fault. Many "dead display" callouts turn out to be a loose cable, a blown fuse, or a mainboard issue —none fixed by a new screen. Work through the checklist below before you condemn the tube.

Common CRT Aging Symptoms

  • Gradual dimming: An aged tube loses roughly 40% of its brightness over its 15,000-hour life; units past 12 years typically sit below 35% brightness —the point where a flashlight no longer helps.
  • Poor focusing: Blurred character edges and distortion make offsets and programs hard to read during programming.
  • Flickering and jitter: Aging high-voltage circuits cause image instability and operator fatigue during prolonged use.
  • Burn-in / ghosting: Permanent phosphor damage from static screens —irreversible on a CRT.

Quick Decision Tree

  1. Does the control power on? If not, check the AC supply, fuse, and PSU first —a dead PSU means no clean 24V reaches the display.
  2. Is the display indicator on? If dark, reseat the HONDA 20-pin and DC 24V leads —a half-seated connector mimics a dead tube.
  3. Is brightness present? Good power rails but a dim or dark screen points to the tube itself.
  4. Is the image normal? Bright but garbled or missing picture points to the CNC or software, not the display.
  5. Is sync stable? Vertical or horizontal roll means HSYNC/VSYNC trouble; if the cable checks out and sync won't lock, replace the display.
⚠ Safety First: A CRT is a high-voltage tube. The anode cap holds 15-28 kV of residual charge long after power-off. Before touching the tube: power down, lock out and tag out the AC disconnect, wait 5-10 minutes, then discharge the anode with a proper insulated high-voltage probe against chassis ground and verify ~0 V with a multimeter. If you are not trained in HV discharge, bring in a qualified technician.

2.3 Signal Compatibility —Why It's Plug-and-Play

Engineers reasonably worry that an LCD won't "speak" the old CRT's video language. With this module it does, and here is the technical reason.

The HONDA 20-pin connector (HRS blue shell) carries everything the display needs: GND, R, G, B, INTENSITY, HSYNC, VSYNC, and DC 24V. There is no separate video board, external scaler, or LVDS bridge to wire —the Kongto LCD accepts that exact pinout on the same connector.

FANUC 9-inch CRTs in the A61L-0001-009X family use TTL/RGB (CGA) monochrome signaling at a line frequency of ~15.7 kHz and a frame rate of 50-60 Hz. The native CRT resolution is 640×400, accepting up to 640×480 over RGB. The Kongto panel converts that old signal path internally (TTL/RGB → VGA → TFT-LCD) and upscales to 800×600 on a modern TFT panel.

Because the conversion happens entirely inside the module, the CNC mainboard still outputs the same HONDA 20-pin TTL/RGB it always did —no parameter to edit, no video mode to select, no handshake. Machine parameters, offsets, and programs stay exactly as they are.

Step 3: Physical Installation Procedure

The entire physical installation takes approximately 30 minutes and requires only a Phillips screwdriver. Follow these detailed steps:

1 Power Off

  • Turn off the CNC system main power and confirm the electrical cabinet power indicator is off
  • Wait at least 5-10 minutes for the CRT high-voltage anode residual charge to bleed off (see safety note below)
  • If available, use a multimeter to verify zero voltage at power terminals before proceeding
⚠ High-Voltage Safety: A CRT is a high-voltage tube —the anode cap holds 15-28 kV of residual charge long after power-off. Before touching the old tube, lock out and tag out the AC disconnect, wait 5-10 minutes, then discharge the anode with a proper insulated high-voltage probe against chassis ground and verify ~0 V with a multimeter. If you are not trained in HV discharge, bring in a qualified technician.

2 Remove Old CRT

  • Use a Phillips screwdriver to remove the 4 corner mounting screws from the display bezel
  • Carefully remove the CRT display from its mounting position —note the CRT is heavy (approx. 2-3 kg), support the bottom with one hand
  • Disconnect the rear HONDA 20-pin signal cable —press the latch to release, then pull straight out
  • Disconnect the DC 24V 2-pin power cable
  • Keep the removed screws safely —they will be reused for the new module

3 Install LCD Upgrade Module

  • Place the Kongto LCD module into the original CRT mounting position, aligning all four mounting holes
  • The LCD module housing has foolproof alignment tabs —it will not fit if oriented incorrectly
  • Tighten the four corner screws with moderate force —do not overtighten

4 Connect Cables

  • Align the HONDA 20-pin signal cable with the LCD module rear signal port —a "click" from the latch confirms proper seating
  • Insert the DC 24V 2-pin power cable into the corresponding power connector
  • Verify both connectors are fully locked in place

5 Power On and Test

  • Restore CNC system main power and power on
  • The LCD module will automatically detect the signal and display —no parameter configuration required
  • Use the OSD menu buttons on the LCD front panel to fine-tune brightness, contrast, and screen position for optimal display
⚠ Note: The entire installation process requires no CNC system parameter modifications, no machine coordinate recalibration, and does not affect machining accuracy. The LCD module is fully transparent and compatible with the CNC system.
