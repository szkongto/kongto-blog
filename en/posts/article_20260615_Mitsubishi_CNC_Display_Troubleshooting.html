Mitsubishi CNC Display Troubleshooting

Your Mitsubishi screen just died. Or it's flickering. Or the colors are wrong. Here's how to figure out what's broken — and whether it's worth fixing.

Home > Posts > Mitsubishi Troubleshooting

Before you do anything: power cycle the machine

I know this sounds obvious. But 10–15% of "display failures" are actually just the CNC controller being confused after a power spike or a dirty shutdown.

Do this first: Power off the machine at the main disconnect. Wait 2 full minutes (not 10 seconds — the capacitors need to discharge). Power back on. If the display comes back — great. But it'll probably happen again. Read on.

Symptom 1: Display is completely black (no power)

What you're seeing

You power on the machine. The CNC controller seems to be running (fans spinning, status LEDs on), but the display is 100% black. No light at all — not even a faint glow.

Most likely causes (in order)

  1. CRT filament burnt out. This is the most common failure on M70/M700 series with original CRTs. The filament has a finite life, and 15+ year-old machines are right at the limit.
  2. Power supply to the monitor failed. The CRT has its own high-voltage power section. If the fuse blew or a capacitor failed, the CRT gets no power even though the CNC controller is fine.
  3. Signal cable disconnected or corroded. Less common, but worth checking — especially in humid environments where the connector pins corrode.

What to check (step by step)

  1. Check the power LED on the display bezel (if equipped). If it's on — the CRT has power but the filament is dead. Replacement time.
  2. Open the electrical cabinet and check the fuse for the display circuit. If it's blown, replace it — but if it blows again immediately, there's a short. Don't keep replacing fuses.
  3. Check the signal cable (HD15 or proprietary Mitsubishi connector). Reseat it on both ends. Look for bent pins or corrosion.

Can it be repaired? Sometimes. A burnt filament means the entire CRT needs replacement. A blown power supply can be repaired, but finding someone who still repairs CRT power supplies is... difficult in 2026. An LCD retrofit kit is usually both cheaper and more reliable than a CRT repair at this point.

Symptom 2: Image is flickering or dim

What you're seeing

The display is on, but the image flickers (especially noticeable on text), or it's noticeably dimmer than it used to be. Or it takes 10+ minutes after power-on to "warm up" to normal brightness.

Most likely causes

  1. CRT near end of life. The phosphor coating on the inside of the CRT tube degrades over time. There's no fix for this — the tube needs replacement.
  2. High-voltage section failing. The flyback transformer or the high-voltage capacitor is degrading. The image may flicker intermittently, then stabilize, then flicker again.
  3. Backlight inverter (if LCD) failing. If you already have an LCD retrofit, the LED backlight or its driver is failing. This is rare (LEDs last a long time), but the driver board can fail.

Quick diagnostic

Take a photo of the screen with your phone. If the photo shows a clear image even though your eyes see a dim/flickering image — the CRT tube is fine, the problem is in the high-voltage section. If the photo also looks dim — the tube itself is dying.

Repair vs. replace: Flickering/dim CRT = replace. The high-voltage section is dangerous to work on (we're talking 15,000+ volts), and even if you fix it, the tube is still old. An LCD retrofit eliminates both problems permanently.

Symptom 3: "No signal" or garbled image

What you're seeing

The display has power, but it says "No signal," shows a completely garbled/jumbled image, or displays only horizontal/vertical lines instead of the CNC interface.

Most likely causes

  1. Signal cable failure. The cable between the CNC controller and the display has broken wires (from years of vibration) or corroded pins.
  2. Video signal board in the CRT has failed. The CRT has a small circuit board that processes the video signal. If this fails, no image gets to the tube.
  3. CNC controller video output failed. Rare, but possible — the video output section on the M70/M700 main board has failed. This is bad news (controller replacement territory).

Diagnostic steps

  1. Try reseating the signal cable on both ends. Power off, disconnect, inspect pins, reconnect firmly. This fixes maybe 20% of "no signal" problems.
  2. If you have a spare display (even a different model), try swapping it in. If the spare works, the problem is in your original display. If the spare also has no signal — the problem is in the CNC controller's video output.

The good news: If the problem is the display (not the controller), an LCD retrofit kit completely replaces the display side. You don't need to troubleshoot further — just swap it out.

Symptom 4: Colors are wrong (pink/green tint, or color shift)

What you're seeing

The image is visible, but the colors are off. Text that should be white looks pink or green. Or the entire image has a color cast that wasn't there before.

Most likely causes

  1. CRT convergence problem. The electron guns inside the CRT are no longer aligned properly. This happens as the CRT ages.
  2. Signal cable has a broken color wire. If the red signal wire breaks, everything looks cyan/green. If the blue wire breaks, everything looks yellow/orange.
  3. Video amplifier circuit failing. The CRT has small circuit boards (sometimes called the "video amp" or "neck board") that can fail partially, causing color problems.

Can it be fixed? Convergence problems in a CRT are not practical to repair in the field. Broken signal cables can be replaced (if you can find the correct Mitsubishi proprietary cable). But again — at this age, replacing the entire display with LCD is the most cost-effective solution.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I just buy a standard computer monitor and connect it?
No. Mitsubishi CNC controllers use a proprietary video signal that a standard VGA/HDMI monitor can't understand. You need a signal converter (which is included in our retrofit kits). Don't waste money on a random monitor — it won't work.
How long does a repair typically take vs. an LCD retrofit?
Repair (if even possible): 1–3 weeks (shipping to repair shop + turnaround). LCD retrofit: 1–2 days (shipping to you) + 1 hour to install. The retrofit wins on downtime every time.
Will the LCD retrofit preserve my existing screen settings/menus?
Yes — the LCD shows exactly the same interface as the original CRT. The signal converter passes through the video signal unchanged. Your CNC menus, parameters, and display settings are all exactly the same. The only difference is the image is sharper and the backlight doesn't flicker.
What if the problem is the CNC controller, not the display?
If you install the LCD kit and still have no image, the problem is almost certainly in the CNC controller's video output section. At that point, you're looking at controller repair or replacement — which is a much bigger expense. But in our experience, 85–90% of "display problems" are actually the display, not the controller.
Do you offer a warranty on the LCD retrofit kits?
Yes — 2 years on the LCD panel and converter board. If it fails (which is rare), we ship a replacement. The LED backlight is rated for 50,000+ hours, so it'll outlast the rest of your machine.
