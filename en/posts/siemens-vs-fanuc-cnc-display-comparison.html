Siemens vs FANUC CNC Display: Which Should You Choose?

Published: June 15, 2026  |  Comparison Siemens FANUC

If you're upgrading a CNC machine's display, or replacing a failed one, you've probably noticed that Siemens SINUMERIK and FANUC dominate the market. Between them, they cover an estimated 60–70% of CNC machines worldwide. But which display ecosystem is better? And if you're switching machines, should you factor display cost and availability into your decision?

We work with both every day. Here's what the data (and our technicians) actually say — not marketing fluff, but real-world experience from hundreds of display replacements.

Quick Verdict (For the Impatient)

Bottom Line First

Choose Siemens SINUMERIK if: You want better networking, more flexible programming, and you're running complex 5-axis or multi-axis work. Display replacement is more expensive but Siemens' modularity means you can sometimes repair individual components.

Choose FANUC if: You prioritize reliability, lowest total cost of ownership, and simplicity. FANUC displays fail less often, and when they do, LCD upgrade kits are widely available and affordable ($200–$600 vs. $500–$1,200 for Siemens).

Detailed Comparison

Key insight: FANUC's lower upgrade cost isn't just about the kit price — it's also about choice. multiple suppliers make LCD upgrade kits for FANUC displays, which keeps prices competitive. Siemens kits have fewer suppliers, so prices are higher.

2. Display Quality & User Experience

Siemens SINUMERIK (6FC3988 / SM0901):

  • Brighter out of the box — Siemens displays typically have better factory brightness specs
  • Touch option — the SM0901 includes resistive touch, which some operators prefer for manual data input
  • Wider viewing angle — Siemens LCD panels (when upgraded) have better off-angle visibility
  • Drawback: SM0901 touch calibration drifts over time; requires periodic recalibration

FANUC (A61L-0001 series):

  • Simpler interface — no touch to fail; dedicated MDI keypad is reliable and familiar
  • Faster boot — FANUC displays initialize faster after power-on (5–10 seconds vs. 15–30 for Siemens)
  • Drawback: Lower resolution on older models (640×480 on some A61L-0001-007x units) — text can look pixelated

4. Upgrade & Repair Ecosystem

This is where FANUC pulls ahead. Because FANUC displays (especially the A61L-0001 series) were produced in such high volumes, the aftermarket is robust:

  • Multiple upgrade kit suppliers — you're not locked into one vendor
  • DIY-friendly — many FANUC LCD upgrade kits include detailed photo instructions; competent maintenance staff can install in under an hour
  • Universal mounting — A61L-0001 series uses relatively standard VESA-style mounting; Siemens uses proprietary mounts that vary by SINUMERIK version

Siemens isn't bad — we make excellent upgrade kits for Siemens too — but you'll have fewer supplier options and the installations are sometimes more complex.

Can't Decide? Here's Our Honest Recommendation

You Probably Don't Have a Choice

Here's the thing — if your machine came with Siemens SINUMERIK, you're not going to swap it for a FANUC display. The control systems are completely different. The "vs" comparison matters mostly when:

  • You're buying a new machine and choosing between Siemens-controlled vs. FANUC-controlled versions
  • You're retrofitting an old machine and have some flexibility in control choice
  • You're replacing a failed display and wondering if there's a better alternative (short answer: usually not — stick with what your machine was designed for)

If you're just replacing a failed display — call us. We'll tell you exactly which upgrade kit fits your machine, usually in under 2 minutes.
