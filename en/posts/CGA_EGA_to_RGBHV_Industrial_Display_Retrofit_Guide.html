7. Step-by-Step Installation Guide

This expanded installation guide covers the complete process from power-down to final verification. Follow each step carefully to ensure a safe, reliable retrofit.

Step 1: Power Down and Lockout/Tagout (LOTO)

Shut down the CNC machine tool using the standard shutdown procedure. Disconnect main power at the disconnect switch. Apply a lockout/tagout device to prevent accidental re-energization. Verify zero voltage using a calibrated multimeter at the power input terminals. Wait at least 2 minutes for internal capacitors to discharge 鈥?CRT power supplies can store lethal voltages even after power-off.

WARNING: CRT flyback transformers and power supply capacitors can retain dangerous voltages for several minutes after power-off. Always verify with a meter before touching any internal components.

Step 2: Access and Isolate the Original CRT

Open the electrical cabinet or display enclosure. Locate the original CRT display unit. Document the existing cable routing with photographs. Disconnect the signal cable from the CNC controller to the CRT 鈥?note which connector pins carry R, G, B, H-sync, and V-sync signals. Disconnect the CRT power cable (typically 24V DC or 100-240V AC depending on the display). Label all cables with adhesive tags before disconnecting.

If the CRT is physically mounted inside the enclosure, remove the mounting screws. Be prepared for the CRT's weight (5-12 kg typical). Use a second person or a mechanical lift for larger units.

Step 3: Mount the Signal Converter

Select a mounting location for the Kongto JTX-CVR-001 converter inside the electrical cabinet. The ideal location is:

  • On a DIN rail (if the converter includes a DIN rail clip) or on a flat surface using the mounting ears
  • Away from high-heat sources such as power supplies and servo drives
  • Away from high-EMI sources such as variable frequency drives (VFDs) and contactors (maintain at least 15 cm clearance)
  • In a position that allows access to all connector ports
  • With adequate airflow around the converter housing

Secure the converter using M4 or M5 screws (depending on mounting bracket) or snap it onto the DIN rail. Ensure the converter is oriented with the ventilation slots unobstructed.

Step 4: Connect the CGA/EGA Input Signal

Route the CGA/EGA signal cable from the CNC controller to the converter's input connector. Refer to the connector pinout documented in Step 2 and the Connector Type Reference Table above. Typical pin assignments:

CGA/EGA to JTX-CVR-001 Input Wiring (HONDA 20-pin example):
Pin 1: Red Video — Connect to converter Red input
Pin 2: Green Video — Connect to converter Green input
Pin 3: Blue Video — Connect to converter Blue input
Pin 4: Composite Sync / H-Sync — Connect to converter H-Sync input
Pin 5: V-Sync (EGA only) — Connect to converter V-Sync input
Pin 6: Ground — Connect to converter GND
Pins 14-15: +12V / +5V (do not connect — for original CRT only)

For CGA signals with composite sync, the converter automatically extracts H and V sync internally. For TTL-level signals (5V logic), ensure the converter's input impedance is set for TTL (75-ohm termination with series resistor). The JTX-CVR-001 handles this automatically via its auto-sensing input stage.

Use twisted-pair cable for the RGB lines if extending beyond 1 meter. Keep the cable run as short as possible 鈥?ideally under 3 meters for reliable signal integrity.

Step 5: Connect the RGBHV Output to the New Display

Run a standard VGA cable (HD-15 male-to-male) from the converter's RGBHV output port to the replacement industrial LCD or LED display. For long runs (over 5 meters), use a high-quality shielded VGA cable with ferrite cores. Alternatively, use 5 individual BNC cables (R, G, B, H, V) if your replacement display has BNC inputs 鈥?this provides superior signal quality at longer distances.

If the replacement display supports DVI or HDMI input, you may need a VGA-to-DVI or VGA-to-HDMI adapter cable. Note that these adapters must be active (not passive) types for reliable signal conversion from analog to digital.

Step 6: Power Connection

Connect the converter's power input (24V DC nominal). Observe proper polarity: connect the positive (+) lead to the V+ terminal and the negative (-) lead to the GND terminal. The converter is reverse-polarity protected, but always verify polarity before power-on. The converter draws less than 2W (typical 100 mA at 24V DC).

If the CNC system provides a 24V DC supply in the cabinet, you can tap into an existing terminal block. Use a dedicated fuse (500 mA slow-blow recommended) for the converter power circuit to allow for independent troubleshooting.

Step 7: Initial Power-On and Signal Verification

Remove all lockout/tagout devices. Re-energize the CNC system. Observe the following in sequence:

  1. The converter's power LED should illuminate (green). If not, verify power connection.
  2. The converter's signal lock LED should illuminate (green) within 2-3 seconds after the CNC controller boots. If flashing red, the input signal format is not detected 鈥?re-check wiring.
  3. The replacement display should show the CNC control interface. If the display shows "No Signal," check the VGA cable connection at both ends and verify the display's input source selection.
  4. If the image is distorted (rolling, split, or offset), the horizontal or vertical sync timing may need adjustment. Most modern LCDs auto-sync to 60 Hz; if the converter's output is set to a non-standard refresh, adjust the DIP switches on the JTX-CVR-001 (see converter manual).
Tip: If the display shows a "Frequency Out of Range" message, power off, set the converter's output to 640x480@60Hz via the DIP switch, and power on again. This is the safest fallback resolution for CGA conversions.

For FANUC systems, you may need to adjust the following FANUC parameters if the display behaves unexpectedly after the retrofit:

  • Parameter 9100 (Display type) — set to match new display
  • Parameter 9110-9115 (Display coordinates) — adjust if image is clipped
  • Parameter 9301 (Screen saver timeout) — set appropriately for LCD

Refer to your specific CNC controller's maintenance manual before making parameter changes. Always record original values before modifying.

Step 9: Secure All Cables and Close the Cabinet

Once signal verification is complete and all parameters are confirmed:

  • Use cable ties to secure all wiring along existing cable routes. Do not route signal cables parallel to high-power AC cables (maintain at least 10 cm separation).
  • Label both ends of every cable with the signal name (e.g., "CGA Input," "RGBHV Output," "24V Power").
  • Dress cables so they do not obstruct ventilation fans or filter panels.
  • Close and secure the electrical cabinet. Apply any panel gaskets or seals that were removed during access.

Step 10: 4-Hour Burn-In and Final Verification

Allow the system to run for a minimum of 4 hours under normal operating conditions. During this burn-in period, check the following every 30 minutes:

  • Display image stability (no flicker, no rolling, no sync loss)
  • Converter surface temperature (should remain below 50°C)
  • No alarm messages on the CNC controller related to display or video
  • All CNC functions accessible and read correctly on the new display

After the burn-in period, document the completed installation with photographs. Update the machine maintenance log with the retrofit date, converter model and serial number, and any parameter changes made.

9. Performance Evaluation & Maintenance

After completing the retrofit and burn-in period, verify the following performance indicators:

  • Display stability: no flicker, no signal loss during 8-hour continuous operation
  • Image quality: clear text, accurate colors, no ghosting
  • Temperature: converter surface temperature below 50°C under load
  • EMI compliance: no interference with other CNC subsystems

Maintenance Schedule: Inspect connections every 6 months. Clean dust from converter ventilation openings annually. Expected converter service life: 5+ years.
