What Is the Mitsubishi FCUA-CT100?

The FCUA-CT100 is a CRT-based industrial display unit that Mitsubishi supplied with their M500 and M520 CNC systems. If you've got a machine with this unit, you'll recognize the problem: the CRT tube dims over time, the image flickers, and eventually — it doesn't come on at all.

These displays were built in the 1990s and early 2000s. At this point, most of them are 20+ years old. Even if the CRT still works, it's living on borrowed time. When it fails, you're looking at a machine down — and Mitsubishi doesn't make replacements anymore.

That's where our TFT LCD retrofit kit comes in. It's designed to be a direct mechanical and electrical replacement for the FCUA-CT100. Same mounting points. Same signal interface. Same 5V/12V power. The difference is the display technology: a modern TFT LCD panel that's brighter, sharper, and built to last another 15+ years.

Why Upgrade From CRT?

Shops sometimes ask us: "The CRT still works, should I wait until it dies?" Here's why that's usually a bad idea:

  • Gradual failure is hard to spot. A CRT doesn't usually go from "perfect" to "dead" overnight. The brightness drops so slowly that operators adapt to it — until someone new sits at the machine and can't read the screen. By then, you've already been losing precision to operator eye strain.
  • Unscheduled downtime is expensive. When a CRT fails mid-shift, you're not just replacing a display — you're losing hours of machine time. A planned upgrade costs the same but happens on your schedule, not the machine's.
  • CRTs are power hungary. A typical CRT display draws 40-60W. A TFT LCD replacement draws 12-18W. It's not going to pay for itself in energy savings alone, but it's one less thing drawing heat into your electrical cabinet.
  • LCD panels are available; CRTs aren't. You might get lucky on eBay today. You won't in two years. If your machine is critical to production, upgrading before you're forced to is just good planning.

Installation Process

The installation takes about 30-45 minutes. You don't need an electronics background — if you can use a screwdriver and identify a connector, you can do this.

What's in the box

  • TFT LCD panel (mounted in a metal enclosure that matches the FCUA-CT100 form factor)
  • Signal interface board (handles the CRT-to-LCD signal conversion internally)
  • Power cable (plugs into the same power connector as the original)
  • Mounting hardware (screws and brackets — usually you can reuse the originals, but we include spares)
  • Installation guide (with photos, not just diagrams)

Step-by-step

  1. Power down the CNC system. Wait 5 minutes for capacitors to discharge. This is safety, not optional.
  2. Remove the CRT unit. Unscrew the mounting brackets (usually 4 screws). Carefully pull the unit forward — there's a video cable and a power cable connected to the back. Disconnect both. Note: take a photo of the cable connections before you disconnect anything. It's obvious in retrospect, but people forget.
  3. Install the LCD unit. Connect the video and power cables to the new unit. Mount it using the same screw holes. Hand-tighten first, then torque to spec (don't overtighten — you're mounting into metal, not stripping threads).
  4. Power up and test. Turn the system on. The display should light up during the CNC boot sequence. Check that the image is stable (no flicker, no wavy lines). If something looks off, power down and check the cable connections — 90% of "installation problems" are loose cables.

No software configuration. No parameter changes. The CNC system doesn't "know" the display changed — it's still sending the same video signal, and the new unit is still displaying it.

FAQ

Will this work if my FCUA-CT100 has a slightly different sub-model number?

Probably. Mitsubishi used several sub-model variants of the FCUA-CT100, but the signal interface and mechanical mounting are standardized across the series. Send us your exact model number and we'll confirm — usually within a few hours.

Do I need to change any CNC parameters after installation?

No. The TFT upgrade kit is electrically transparent to the CNC system. The system sends the same video signal it always has; the upgrade kit converts and displays it. No parameter changes, no software updates.

How long does the TFT panel last compared to the CRT?

A CRT display has a typical lifespan of 15,000-20,000 hours of actual use (less if the brightness is turned up). A TFT LCD panel is rated for 50,000+ hours. In practical terms: the CRT might last 5-8 years in a single-shift shop; the TFT will last 15+ years under the same conditions.
